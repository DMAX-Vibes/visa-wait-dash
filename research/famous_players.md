# Famous Musicians Who Played Yamaha FG (and Yamaha Acoustic) Guitars
*Research compiled 2026-05-14. All claims flagged by confidence level. Unverified claims are explicitly noted.*

---

## Confidence Key

- **HIGH** — Multiple independent sources confirm (photographs, primary interviews, concert film, liner notes, auction provenance)
- **MEDIUM** — One credible source, or multiple secondary sources without a confirmed primary source
- **LOW** — Single secondary source, rumor, or claim that could not be independently verified
- **NOT CONFIRMED** — No credible evidence found; claim should not be repeated without a primary source

## Requested Artists — Detailed Findings

---

### Elliott Smith
**Confidence: HIGH**

Smith's primary acoustic was a **Yamaha FG-180**, specifically a Taiwan-manufactured unit (not the more collectible Japanese "Red Label" version, despite frequent confusion in listings). He installed a **Bill Lawrence A-300** soundhole pickup. The guitar was used to record his debut solo album *Roman Candle* (1994) on a four-track Tascam Portastudio 424 in a basement. It was also his primary live acoustic throughout most of his career.

Producer **Rob Schnapf**, in *Tape Op* magazine (Issue 9), recalled *Either/Or* (1997): *"Either/Or was the Yamaha — can't remember the model number."*

**Sources:**
- Photograph at the Academy Awards (Oscars) — Sweetadeline press archive
- Photograph showing the Bill Lawrence A-300 pickup installation — Wennermedia
- Producer Rob Schnapf, *Tape Op* magazine, Issue 9 (primary interview)
- Equipboard: equipboard.com/pros/elliott-smith
- obscurelliott.tumblr.com/post/78041840540
- Harmony Central forum: harmonycentral.com/forums/topic/1150168

**Note:** FG-180s are now routinely described on Reverb as "the Elliott Smith guitar," reflecting how thoroughly this association has entered guitar culture.

---

### Country Joe McDonald (Woodstock, 1969)
**Confidence: HIGH**

McDonald was pushed onstage at Woodstock as an **unplanned solo filler** by festival promoter John Morris — his band (Country Joe and the Fish) was not scheduled until the following day. A stagehand found a **Yamaha FG-150** backstage. When McDonald said he had no strap, a piece of rope was cut from the stage rigging and tied to the guitar. He performed a nine-song solo set including "The Fish Cheer / I-Feel-Like-I'm-Fixin'-To-Die Rag," one of Woodstock's most iconic moments.

**Sources:**
- Country Joe McDonald's own account: countryjoe.com/woodstock.htm (primary)
- Rolling Stone interview (2019) confirming the unplanned nature of the set
- Acoustic Guitar magazine: "Cataloging the Acoustic Guitars at Woodstock"
- Woodstock Elders 1969 Facebook post identifying the FG-150 specifically
- Reverb.com listings for the FG-150 routinely use the nickname "The Country Joe Woodstock Model"
- The Unique Guitar Blog: uniqueguitar.blogspot.com

**Model note:** Some sources have confused the FG-150 with the FG-180. The weight of evidence — including McDonald's own accounts and the Woodstock Elders post — points to the **FG-150**.

### Bruce Springsteen
**Confidence: NOT CONFIRMED**

No credible source links Springsteen to a Yamaha FG or any Yamaha acoustic. His documented acoustic guitars are **Takamine** (his primary acoustic since the early 1980s, including the *Nebraska* sessions with a Takamine EF341SC) and earlier **Martin** instruments. One search result mentioned a "Yamaha FG800 in connection with Springsteen" but provided no citation or evidence.

**Sources checked:** Equipboard, Chicago Music Exchange gear feature, Guitar Lobby, Gearnews, UberProAudio, Rolling Stone, Wildwood Guitars.

**Verdict:** Do not publish a Springsteen/Yamaha FG connection without a verified primary source.

### Paul Simon
**Confidence: HIGH (for Yamaha custom); LOW (for any FG-series)**

Simon has a well-documented Yamaha association, but his guitars were **bespoke custom-shop instruments**, not production FG models.

Beginning in the mid-1970s, Simon worked with master luthier **Terumi "Terry" Nakamoto** at Yamaha's Hamamatsu custom shop to build a series of **black custom LS400 guitars**. The earliest had a white pickguard, later changed to transparent. These guitars appear prominently during the *Graceland* era (1986) and the 1991 Central Park concert.

Yamaha subsequently produced a retail replica designated the **FJ681 "Paul Simon Edition"**.

He was also photographed with a **Yamaha FG720S** at The Gorge in 1999 (one documented sighting; confidence on that specific model is MEDIUM).

**Sources:**
- Brad Priddy's Paul Simon guitar reference: bradpriddy.com/paul_simon/guitar.htm
- paul-simon.info fan forum
- Acoustic Guitar Forum thread: acousticguitarforum.com/forums/showthread.php?t=283504
- Wikimedia Commons category: "Yamaha Paul Simon model"
- The Bluegrass Situation

## Additional Well-Documented Yamaha Players (From General Research)

---

### John Denver — Yamaha L-53 Custom
**Confidence: HIGH**

Denver was one of Yamaha's first major American celebrity custom-shop clients. In 1977 he ordered **Yamaha L-53 custom guitars** designed by luthier **Terumi Nakamoto**, featuring Hokkaido spruce tops and Brazilian rosewood. He used them extensively in concert and on television through the early 1980s.

Documented on-screen uses include the 1979 TV special *John Denver and the Ladies*, the 1979 Muppets special *A Christmas Together* (guitar visible on album cover), and the 1981 Aspen Music Festival. One of Denver's L-53 guitars sold at **Julien's Auctions** with full provenance documentation.

**Sources:**
- *Acoustic Guitar* magazine: "Great Acoustics: John Denver's Luxurious 1978 Yamaha L-53"
- Julien's Auctions catalog (with provenance): bid.juliensauctions.com
- Gruhn Guitars news release
- The Bluegrass Situation

---

### James Taylor — Yamaha FG-2000 and L-55 Custom
**Confidence: MEDIUM–HIGH**

Taylor used the **FG-2000** and the **L-55 Custom** live and in the studio during the 1970s. His 1974 album *Walking Man* is cited in connection with the FG-2000. Taylor also participated in a Yamaha "Me and My Guitars" video series and appeared on Yamaha USA's AllAccess artist page.

**Sources:**
- The Unique Guitar Blog
- The Bluegrass Situation
- Acoustic Guitar Forum discussions
- Yamaha USA artist page: yamaha.com/allaccess/artists/issue13-taylor_james.asp

**Caveat:** Specific liner-note or primary-interview verification for the FG-2000 on *Walking Man* has not been confirmed in this research. Confidence is based on multiple corroborating secondary sources.

---

### Bob Dylan — Yamaha L-52 Custom and L-6 ("Budokan guitars")
**Confidence: HIGH**

During his **1978 world tour** — recorded and released as *Bob Dylan at Budokan* — Dylan used a **Yamaha L-6** as his primary acoustic and a specially ordered **custom L-52** (black, with white square pickguard) for the Japanese shows. The L-52 is now known as "the Dylan Budokan guitar" and is considered one of the most historically significant Japanese acoustic guitars ever made. The concerts are professionally filmed, and the guitars are visually documentable.

In **1986** (the True Confessions Tour with Tom Petty), Dylan is documented playing a **Yamaha FG-1500**.

**Sources:**
- CoastSonic feature on the L-52: coastsonic.com/blogs/news/for-the-collectors-1978-yamaha-custom-l-52
- bobdylansgear.blogspot.com: "BOB DYLAN'S GEAR: YAMAHA L-6 1978"
- The Unique Guitar Blog
- Multiple gear databases; concert film (*At Budokan*, 1979)

**Note:** The Budokan instruments are custom-shop pieces, not production FG guitars, but this is one of the most historically significant Yamaha acoustic guitar stories in rock history.

### John Lennon — Yamaha CJ-52 "Dragon" Custom
**Confidence: HIGH**

During a 1977 vacation in Japan, Lennon met with Yamaha craftsmen. Impressed by Paul Simon's custom Yamaha, he ordered a **CJ-52-based instrument** featuring elaborate Maki-e lacquer inlay: a dragon (drawn by Lennon himself) on the body, a yin-yang symbol below the bridge, and the Chinese character for "dragon" on the headstock. It is considered the most expensive guitar Yamaha's custom shop has ever produced and is now **displayed at the John Lennon Museum in Tokyo**.

**Sources:**
- The Bluegrass Situation
- tcgakki.com Yamaha legend history
- *Yamaha Guitars History* book (inducted into the National Library of Japan)
- John Lennon Museum, Tokyo
- Times Square Chronicles

**Note:** A custom instrument, not an FG-series guitar — but one of the most significant Yamaha acoustic artifacts in music history.

### Bert Jansch — Yamaha FG-1500 and LL11
**Confidence: HIGH** *(includes direct first-person quote)*

After leaving Pentangle in the early 1970s, Jansch had a **formal endorsement contract with Yamaha**. He received an **FG-1500** as his first instrument under the deal, later modifying it (guitar-electronics specialist Red Rhodes cut a hole in the body to install volume controls). He subsequently used a **Yamaha LL11**.

**Direct quote from Bert Jansch** (*Fretboard Journal* interview):
> "After I left Pentangle I had a contract with Yamaha, and the first thing they gave me was this FG1500."

**Sources:**
- *Fretboard Journal* interview with Bert Jansch (primary source)
- Equipboard: equipboard.com/pros/bert-jansch
- Acoustic Guitar Forum
- Guitar International

---

### John Martyn — Yamaha FG-140 and FG-180
**Confidence: HIGH**

Martyn used a **Yamaha FG** — first an **FG-140**, then an **FG-180** (with the original lacquer rubbed off) — as his primary acoustic from roughly **1969 through 1972/73**, covering albums including *Stormbringer!* (1970) and *Solid Air* (1973). He subsequently switched to a Martin D-28.

This is documented on the **official John Martyn website's guitar history page**, making it one of the most directly sourced FG-series connections in the research.

**Sources:**
- Official John Martyn website equipment history: johnmartyn.com/guitar/a-history-of-johns-guitar-set-ups/ (primary)
- Equipboard
- Guitar International

---

### Chris Bell (Big Star) — Yamaha FG-180
**Confidence: MEDIUM**

Chris Bell used a **1970 Yamaha FG-180** on Big Star's *#1 Record* (1972) and on solo recordings for *I Am the Cosmos*. A contemporaneous account describes Bell buying "a Yamaha dreadnought-style acoustic, which was a good guitar and used extensively on the first LP."

**Sources:**
- Worn Frets: wornfrets.com/the-guitars-of-big-star
- Equipboard

---

### Paul Brady — Yamaha FG-180
**Confidence: MEDIUM**

The Irish singer-songwriter used a **Yamaha FG-180** (reportedly with a yellowish rather than red label, suggesting late-1960s manufacture) primarily for Irish folk performances. It is audible in YouTube footage from 1970s BBC sessions, including a version of "Arthur McBride."

**Sources:**
- Acoustic Guitar Forum: acousticguitarforum.com/forums/showthread.php?t=391449
- YouTube BBC session footage (visual/audio confirmation)

## Key Takeaways for the Article

1. **The FG-150 at Woodstock (1969)** is almost certainly the single most historically significant moment for the Yamaha FG brand — Country Joe McDonald's unplanned solo set is widely cited as the guitar's American breakthrough.

2. **Elliott Smith and the FG-180** is the most thoroughly documented FG-series celebrity association of the modern era. The guitar's identity is now so tied to Smith that FG-180s are routinely marketed as "the Elliott Smith guitar."

3. **Paul Simon, John Lennon, and Bob Dylan** all have strong, high-confidence Yamaha connections, but their instruments were bespoke custom-shop pieces — not production-line FG guitars. Worth distinguishing clearly in the article.

4. **Bert Jansch and John Martyn** represent the strongest British folk-era FG connections. Jansch's association is confirmed by a direct first-person quote in the *Fretboard Journal*.

5. **Bruce Springsteen, Karen Carpenter, Jack Johnson, and Mark Knopfler** — no credible evidence found linking any of these artists to Yamaha FG guitars. Claims circulating online about these artists should not be published without a primary source.
